Firewalls & Rule Order

A firewall is a bouncer with a checklist. See who gets in, who is stopped, and why rule order matters.

What just happened?

The step you are on lights up here.

  1. 0. Rules, top to bottom – The firewall reads rules in order and uses the first one that matches the packet.
  2. 1. Allow the web – Public websites need ports 80 (HTTP) and 443 (HTTPS) open to everyone.
  3. 2. Default deny – A final "deny all" rule blocks anything you did not explicitly allow. This is the safest starting point.
  4. 3. Allow by source – Rules can match who is sending: only the admin's IP may use SSH.
  5. 4. Protect the database – Internal services should be reachable only from the servers that need them.
  6. 5. Order matters – A broad ALLOW above a specific DENY silently disables the DENY.
  7. 6. Stateful inspection – The firewall tracks open connections and allows the replies automatically.

Why it matters

Firewalls are the first line of defence for homes, companies and cloud servers. A tiny rule mistake can expose private systems to the whole internet.

Good habits: default deny, allow only what you need, put specific rules before general ones, review rules regularly, and never expose databases publicly. Firewalls reduce risk but do not replace patching and strong passwords.
