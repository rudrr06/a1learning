VLANs: Splitting One Switch Into Many Networks

One switch, two teams. Watch broadcast noise get contained, then see how traffic crosses over safely.

What just happened?

The step you are on lights up here.

  1. 0. One flat network – By default every port is in one VLAN, so one broadcast domain.
  2. 1. Broadcast noise – Broadcasts reach every port. More devices = more noise and more exposure.
  3. 2. Create VLANs – A VLAN is a logical network. You assign ports to VLAN IDs (10, 20…) in the switch.
  4. 3. Contained – Broadcasts and normal traffic stay inside their own VLAN.
  5. 4. Isolation – Hosts in different VLANs cannot talk directly, even on the same switch.
  6. 5. Inter-VLAN routing – A router (or Layer 3 switch) connects VLANs and can apply firewall rules between them.
  7. 6. 802.1Q tagging – Trunk links carry many VLANs by adding a VLAN tag to each frame.

Why it matters

VLANs split a network by role (staff, guests, servers, cameras) without buying separate switches. They reduce noise and make networks easier to manage.

Security: a guest Wi-Fi in its own VLAN cannot reach company servers. Attackers try "VLAN hopping" (abusing trunk ports), so unused ports should be disabled and trunks locked down.
