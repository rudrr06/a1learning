DNS

Humans use names, computers use numbers. Follow one lookup through the whole DNS system.

What just happened?

The step you are on lights up here.

  1. 1. Ask the resolver – Your PC asks a resolver to do the work. The resolver checks its cache first.
  2. 2. Root server – Root servers point the resolver to the right top-level domain servers.
  3. 3. TLD server – The .com servers point to the name servers of the domain.
  4. 4. Authoritative server – The authoritative server holds the real records and gives the final answer.
  5. 5. Cache and connect – The answer is cached for its TTL, and your browser connects to the IP.

Why it matters

Common record types: A (name to IPv4), AAAA (IPv6), CNAME (alias), MX (mail servers), NS (name servers), TXT (text, often used for SPF and verification).

Attacks: DNS spoofing / cache poisoning puts a fake answer into a resolver so users reach the attacker's IP. DNS tunnelling hides data inside DNS queries. DNS amplification is used for DDoS.

Defences: DNSSEC (digital signatures prove an answer is genuine, but do not encrypt it), DNS over HTTPS/TLS (encrypts queries), and DNS filtering. Hackers also use DNS in recon: enumerating subdomains is a first step in bug bounty hunting.
