Phishing Attack Chain

Phishing tricks a person, not a computer. Follow one attack from start to finish, then see where it can be stopped.

What just happened?

The step you are on lights up here.

  1. 1. Recon – The attacker collects public details about the target so the trap feels believable.
  2. 2. Bait – A fake email that looks official, with urgency and a link.
  3. 3. Click – The link text looks harmless, but the real address belongs to the attacker.
  4. 4. Fake page – A copy of the real login page waits for the victim to type their credentials.
  5. 5. Stolen – The credentials go to the attacker's server, and the victim is sent to the real site so nothing seems wrong.
  6. 6. Takeover – The attacker logs in as the victim and spreads to other accounts.
  7. 7. Defense – Breaking any one link in the chain stops the attack.

Why does security care?

Fooling a person is often easier than breaking software, so phishing is one of the most common ways accounts get stolen.

Related terms: spear phishing (aimed at one person using personal details), smishing (by SMS), vishing (by phone call), whaling (aimed at executives).
